    Changelog¶

    Every Sky Island release for Minecraft 1.20.1, newest first, as published on CurseForge.

    1.0.0 (beta)¶

    Released 2026-10-16 · Download

    Skypiea, complete. God himself waits on the deck of the Ark Maxim as the last Ordeal; the golden Shandora and the great serpent hide on the Upper Yards; the Golden Bell rings and makes peace with the Shandias; the express shrimp carries your boat in, and a balloon octopus brings it home. Eight new dials, the Vearth urn and its sherds, the conch lantern made over, an advancement tab of its own, and the whole mod in French.

    Requires Minecraft 1.20.1, Forge 47+, Mine Mine no Mi 0.11.6 and AkumaLib 4.7.0 or newer. Client and server.

    ⚠️ Update AkumaLib to 4.7.0 before you start: Sky Island 1.0.0 does not load with an older one.

    ⚠️ On a server, the server and every player need Sky Island 1.0.0: a 0.4.0 game cannot join a 1.0.0 server, nor the other way round.

    Worlds are kept. What you have already explored stays as it is; the golden Shandora, the serpent's lairs and the new chests appear in the parts you have not seen yet.

    New

    • Enel, the last Ordeal. Beat the four priests and a fifth Ordeal appears in the challenges menu, under God's Priests: God himself, on the deck of his flying ship, the Ark Maxim. Standard and Hard, as the others.
      • A body of lightning. Blows go through him, and striking him with a hand or a blade shocks you back. He is hurt by Haki (or seastone, or rubber) at any time - and by anything at all for a few seconds after each of his great attacks, while he glows.
      • You see every attack coming. He stands and shows where it will land: a ring on the deck for El Thor, a line for his bolts. Move out of it and you are missed.
      • Gold carries lightning. The deck is wood barred with gold, and the rail is capped with it. When he drives his staff into the deck, the gold crackles, then strikes whoever stands on it: step onto the wood, or jump.
      • Three phases. First his staff, El Thor and Sango. At two thirds of his health, his drums wake their beasts: Hino the bird, fast and burning; Kiten the wolf, which runs after you; Julungul the dragon, long and wide. In the last third, Raigo gathers over the Ark: a countdown at the top of the screen. If he still stands when it runs out, the ball falls on the whole deck and the Ordeal is lost.
      • In Hard, he also turns into Amaru, the giant of lightning, for the whole last phase - and everything is faster.
      • The powers of the Goro Goro no Mi. His lightning is the fruit's own: El Thor's pillar from the sky, Sango's bolts, the great dark ball of Raigo, the giant Amaru. A body of smoke, sand or fire does not protect you from it.
      • What it gives. The gains of a challenge, more than any priest's - and at your first victory, his golden staff.
    • Eight new dials. The dials of daily life in Skypiea, in the look of Mine Mine no Mi's own. Like the others they can be set down as a shell, they stack by sixteen, and the ones you use are spent by it.
      • Lamp Dial: set it down, it gives light.
      • Heat Dial: use it with raw food in your other hand and the whole stack is cooked at once. In a furnace it burns as long as a bucket of lava.
      • Jet Dial: use it and you are thrown far, the way you look. The landing does you no harm.
      • Ball Dial: use it and a ball of cloud forms under your feet and holds you a few seconds - handy to catch a fall.
      • Tone Dial: set it down, it rings when a monster or a player comes near.
      • Water Dial: use it for a jet of water ahead of you: it puts out fire, on you and in front of you, and pushes back whoever stands in it.
      • Flavor Dial: set it down and the animals around come to it, as to their food.
      • Vision Dial: use it to take a photo of what you see. Hold the photo to see it in your hand, hang it in an item frame, or right-click it to look at it full screen. It also keeps where you took it: its coordinates, its land and its world. Every player sees the same picture, even after the photo changes hands.
      • Where to find them: they wash up on the Angel Beaches with the others, and the Skypiean dial merchants sell them. The two rare ones, Jet and Vision, are sold by Birka's dial craftsman.
    • The golden Shandora. One Shandora in twenty is not a ruin: the City of Gold still stands, whole. Same square, same avenues, same halls and towers as the ruins - but with every wall up, gilded stone, roofs and gates of gold, and a clearing kept round it, so it shines out of the jungle.
      • Rich everywhere. Every hall has a chest of gold, diamonds and rare dials, with a box in one chest in two. The crypt under the square gives two boxes a chest, a Golden one half the time, and netherite. About ten boxes in a city.
      • Well kept. The Shandias are many there: their chief and warriors on the square, more in the avenues and in the crypt. They leave you alone as long as you touch nothing; open one chest and the whole city is on you.
      • Found by chance only. No map and no trail tells a golden city from a ruined one: the altars' slabs lead to it as they lead to any Shandora.
      • It appears on Upper Yards not explored yet.
    • The great serpent of Upper Yard. On about one Upper Yard in four, a pit opens in the jungle: the lair of a giant serpent of Nola's kind, the one the Shandias worshipped. A rare boss - one to a lair, and it does not come back once killed.
      • Its lair. A wide pit, stepped all the way round, cleared of trees. In the middle lies the carcass of one of its kind, and the serpent sleeps coiled round it. The Shandias' offering still stands on the rim.
      • A very long body. Its head is only the start of it: strike it anywhere along its body and you hurt it. Stay against its coils and they crush and slow you.
      • It swallows you whole. Now and then its bite takes you inside it: you are carried along and lose health little by little. Strike it from the inside, or have friends strike it from outside - enough blows and it spits you out in front of it. Left alone, it spits you out after a few seconds anyway.
      • What it leaves. Its two fangs, a trophy. And under the carcass's ribs, its treasure: gold, diamonds, extol, a rare dial or an enchanted golden apple - and a Golden Box every time, with a second box beside it.
      • It appears on Upper Yards not explored yet.
    • The express shrimp. Come before Heaven's Gate in a boat and a giant shrimp rises out of the cloud under you, takes the boat on its back, crew aboard, and carries it past the Gate to the open sea by the island's seafront. Nothing to ask and nothing to pay: it is Skypiea's welcome.
    • The balloon octopus: a gentle way down. The seafront's merchant, the one who sells wavers, now sells balloon octopuses too.
      • Click your boat with one in hand: it takes hold of the boat and swells above it.
      • Sail off the edge of the cloud sea: instead of dropping like a stone, the boat comes down slowly under the octopus, crew aboard, all the way to the world below, at the place you left from. Nobody is hurt and the boat does not break, on water or on land.
      • One octopus, one descent: once the boat has touched down, the octopus lets its air out and goes home. An octopus whose boat is broken before it ever served gives itself back.
      • It works on chest boats and wavers too.
      • ⚠️ Without an octopus, the landing hurts. A boat that falls out of the sky bare breaks when it reaches the water or the ground (you get the boat back as an item), and everyone aboard is hurt by how far it fell - the whole way down from Skypiea is deadly for a newcomer. Good armour softens it, and so does the health that Doriki gives: a strong, well-armoured pirate can walk away from it.
      • With Valkyrien Skies: click any block of your ship with an octopus in hand, and a great one takes hold of the whole ship. ⚠️ A ship now needs one to come down safely. Until now a ship that fell out of Skypiea was simply put back on the sea below; from this version it comes back high in the sky, like a boat: under its octopus it floats down slowly with its crew; without one it falls, and when it stops its crew takes the same blow as a boat's (the ship itself stays whole).
    • The Golden Bell rings. Climb the Giant Jack, stand under the bell and click it: its voice carries over the whole island, to everyone on it.
      • It makes peace. The first time you ring an island's bell, the Shandias of that Upper Yard hold nothing against you any more - not what you looted before, not what you take after. They still defend themselves if you strike them.
      • The peace is yours for good, death included, and it is that island's only: another Upper Yard has its own bell, or none.
    • The Vearth urn, made over. The urn now has the shape of Minecraft's decorated pot, fired in the brown earth of the Vearth, with the Vearth still in its mouth.
      • Ten sherds of Skypiea to put on its sides: bell, serpent, ark, geyser, cloud, conch, thunder, bird, Shandia frieze and beanstalk, in gold on the brown clay. Craft the urn like a decorated pot, a brick or a sherd on each side, with dirt in the middle. Only these sherds go on it, not Minecraft's.
      • Where to find them. In the chests of Shandora, of its small ruins and of the Shandia camps, in the serpent's lair, at God's shrine and at the altars. Brush the gravel among the paving of the small ruins. The Skypiean merchants sell the cloud, conch, bird and geyser ones.
      • The urns of villages and temples, and those already in your world, now wear a few sherds of their own. A broken urn keeps its sides.
    • The conch lantern, made over. It is now a real conch, spire up, lit from inside its mouth, and its mouth turns to you when you set it down. Hung from a ceiling, it hangs upside down.
      • Sixteen colours. Put a lantern and a dye in the crafting table to colour it, and again to change the colour. A new lantern has the natural colour of a shell.
      • A new recipe. A conch and a Lamp Dial make a lantern, lit already. Conches wash up on the Angel Beaches with the dials.
      • In villages, a house's lanterns take the colour of its paint.
      • The hole in the old lantern's texture is gone.
    • An advancement tab of its own, Sky Island. A thread through Skypiea, in the order you meet it: the way up, Heaven's Gate and the express shrimp, a dial, a waver, a tamed bird and a ride on it, Weatheria, Birka, the Giant Jack and its bell, Shandora, the steles, each priest beaten in Standard and in Hard, the Sanctuary, and the way back under an octopus. Three stay hidden until you get them. None gives a reward.
    • Sky Island in French. Everything you read has its French text, with the names of the official French version of One Piece: Ener, Bilca, the Prélats and their Ordalies, the Porte du Paradis, the Homard Express, the Octo-Ballon, the Cloche d'Or, the Arche Maxime, the Visio-Dial...

    Changes

    • The Shandias' weapons look like the anime. The Shandia spear is a wooden spear with a big leaf-shaped steel blade, the Heat Javelin has a red-hot point that glows in the dark, the Burn Bazooka is a long dark tube held in both hands, the Shandia shield is round, red and gold. Their icons, the great serpent's fang and the balloon octopus have new icons in Mine Mine no Mi's style.
    • The creative inventory is in order. The Skypiea tab is sorted: clouds and terrain, the villages' blocks, Bilca, the Upper Yard and Shandora, furniture and sherds, the dials, the boats, the weapons. The spawn eggs moved to Minecraft's Spawn Eggs tab, grouped by people.

    Fixes

    • A chest boat or a waver that fell under the cloud sea never came back to the world below: it does now, like any boat.
    • In multiplayer, a player who left the game while a boat came down under its balloon octopus, someone else still aboard, came back alone in the air and fell to their death. They are now back in the boat - or brought down slowly if it has landed meanwhile.
    • At the weather vendor's booth, when another player buys a weather, your open window now greys its buttons at once.
    • The balloon octopus no longer shakes above its boat on the way down.

    Improved

    • Birkan towns load faster. Walking into a living town of Birka for the first time makes the game work about a quarter less.

    0.4.0 (beta)¶

    Released 2026-10-09 · Download

    Enel's world. The Upper Yards get their altars and the great Sacrificial Altar on its lake, the four Ordeals of God's priests and the Sanctuary of God with its divine soldiers; and a third kind of island appears in the sky, Birka, Enel's homeland - in ruins round its crater, or, rarely, a whole town with its people, its merchant and its craftsman of dials. Finds are rarer on the Upper Yards, and a slowdown of the whole server is fixed.

    Requires Minecraft 1.20.1, Forge 47+, Mine Mine no Mi 0.11.5 and AkumaLib 4.3.0 or newer. Client and server.

    ⚠️ Update AkumaLib to 4.3.0 before you start: Sky Island 0.4.0 does not load with an older one.

    Worlds are kept. What you have already explored stays as it is; the new islands, altars, steles and structures appear in the parts you have not seen yet.

    New

    • Sacrificial altars on the Upper Yards. Two or three on an island, in clearings of the jungle: a small court of stone, a table of offerings between two urns of Vearth, two braziers kept burning, and a gate.
      • They are a trail. A few gilded slabs leave each altar towards the next one: follow them from altar to altar and you reach Shandora, the City of Gold - or the Giant Jack on an island that has no city.
      • The offerings are modest: Vearth, gold nuggets, some fruit, now and then a pouch of extol. No boxes.
      • They belong to the Shandias. Take the offerings or break the altar with warriors nearby, and they turn on you as they do for a looter of Shandora.
    • The great Sacrificial Altar, on about one Upper Yard in three: a stepped altar of old stone standing in the middle of a lake of cloud.
      • There is no bridge. Swim, sail or fly across - and sky sharks guard the lake: they circle low over the cloud and go for whoever comes near.
      • A great stair rises out of the lake to the offerings on top: gold, extol, fruit, and a box in about one chest in two (Wooden or Iron, a Golden one now and then).
      • It is part of the altars' trail. Through the gate at its back, two gilded posts stand on the far shore: the trail goes on from there, towards the City of Gold. On an island with neither a city nor a Giant Jack, the small altars lead to it instead.
    • The Ordeals of God's priests begin: the Ordeal of Balls. Enel's priests are challenges, started from the challenges menu like those of the Blue Sea, under a category of their own, God's Priests. The first one is Satori, in his forest of Surprise Clouds.
      • You learn of an Ordeal in Skypiea only, from its stele. An Upper Yard has the steles of two of the four Ordeals, on its plains - the others are on other islands, so learning them all takes more than one Upper Yard: a paved enclosure of stone with a great poneglyph at its middle, and four pillars that bear the Ordeal's mark - balls of cloud for Satori's, chains for Shura's, puddles of swamp cloud for Gedatsu's, crowns of barbed wire for Ohm's. Rub the poneglyph with paper, as you would any poneglyph, and the Ordeal is yours. No poster and no poneglyph of the Blue Sea ever gives one. (The steles stand on Upper Yards not explored yet.)
      • The forest is the fight. A clearing among giant trees on their mossy roots, a Milky Road winding between them - and balls of cloud of every size floating all over it. Nobody knows what is in one until it opens: flowers, a songbird, a venomous snake, blades, flame or powder. Touch one or strike it, and it opens on whoever stands near.
      • Satori reads your blows and is not there. He bounds away, keeps his distance, and sends his clouds at you with his cane. Half beaten, he calls the Ball Dragon: a long string of clouds full of powder behind a festival dragon's mask, big at the head and small at the tail, that comes for you - strike any ball of it and the whole string goes up.
      • Standard and Hard, as every challenge: beat him once and the Hard Ordeal opens - more health, haki, stronger clouds, two dragons, and the dragon breathes fire. It pays as the other challenges do, with a dial now and then (Impact, Breath or Flame).
    • The Ordeal of String: Shura, the second of the priests, on the back of his giant bird Fuza.
      • Its stele is the one with chains strung from pillar to pillar.
      • The forest is strung with String Cloud: threads so thin you barely see them, between the trunks, at the knee and at the chest. Walk through one and it wraps round you. A few and you slow down; more, and you are bound - unable to move or strike for a moment. Go crouching and they do not catch you; leave them alone and they fall off.
      • Shura fights from the air. Fuza dives at you for his master's Heat Javelin - whatever the lance strikes burns - and at once when the strings hold you, when it bites half again as deep. The bird also breathes a long jet of fire, which burns the strings in its way - and opens you a path through them.
      • Bring the bird down, or hurt Shura enough, and he comes down to finish it on foot: he lunges with his lance, and casts more string at you from afar. When he falls, Fuza flies away.
      • Standard and Hard, as the Ordeal of Balls; a dial now and then (Axe, Breath or Flame).
    • The Ordeal of Swamp: Gedatsu, the third of the priests.
      • Its stele is the one with puddles of lilac cloud at the foot of its pillars.
      • The whole arena is pitted with Swamp Cloud: pools of thick lilac cloud sunk in the ground, from the clearing to the walls. Step in one and you sink, slowly, and you wade rather than walk. Once your head is under you are Smothered: you see nothing but lilac, your breath is gone in three seconds, and your health follows. Struggle up and toward the bank - hold jump - and you climb out; keep still - crouch - and you stop sinking.
      • Gedatsu walks on his swamp: the Milky Dials in his shoes bear him. His Jet Punch throws him at you from afar and sends you flying, straight back - mind what lies behind you. You hear the dial hiss first. From afar he stands still for a moment and a thread of cloud on the ground shows the line he will fly along: step aside of it, and he flies past. At arm's length, step out of his reach before the blow, and it misses. His Swamp Cloud Burger is a ball of swamp cloud thrown on your head: Smothered for a few seconds, wherever you stand. He winds it up too: be out of his sight when he lets go, and it misses.
      • He is careless. A punch that finds nothing leaves him standing for a moment, and now and then he rolls his eyes so far back that he cannot see: your opening.
      • Standard and Hard, as the other Ordeals; a dial now and then (Milky or Breath).
    • The Ordeal of Iron: Ohm, the last and the strongest of the priests, with his giant dog Holy - three stars where the other Ordeals have two, and a richer reward.
      • Its stele is the one with crowns of barbed wire on its pillars.
      • The White-Barbed Iron Deathmatch: a dome of barbed Iron Cloud shuts the clearing from the first moment. Touch the wire and it pricks you. Each time Ohm has lost a quarter of his health the dome draws in - a ring of sparks shows where it will stand - until there is hardly room left to fight.
      • Iron Plates lie on the turf, plain to see. Step on one - or be thrown on one - and barbed wire springs out all round it: it stabs whoever is there and shuts you in for a few seconds. Then the plate is ready again. Ohm and Holy walk over them unharmed.
      • Ohm fights with the Eisen Whip, a sword whose blade is Iron Cloud. From afar he shows a line, then the blade runs out along it, a dozen blocks: step out of the line. And his Eisen Back throws a wall of wire up between you when he sees a blow or a shot coming: it takes the blow, and stands a moment.
      • Holy charges and sends you flying, and bites like the Giant Dog he is. But he obeys anyone: crouch before him, looking at him, and he sits for a few seconds. He will not be told twice in a row. When his master falls, he gives up.
      • Standard and Hard; a dial now and then (Eisen or Milky).
    • The Sanctuary of God, Enel's residence, on the summit of every Upper Yard that has neither a Giant Jack nor the City of Gold - a little over one island in four.
      • A great stair of cloud climbs the flank, from the jungle to an esplanade of white stone lined with braziers: it follows the mountainside, gentle where the slope is gentle and steep where it is steep, and it is climbed without a jump. At the esplanade's back, under a wide red roof with upturned corners and the plaque of God on its front, stands the hall with its pink curtains.
      • Inside, the throne, and behind it the ring of Thunder Drums - strike one and it sounds.
      • The treasure lies at the foot of the throne, as rich as the chest under the Golden Bell: one or two boxes every time - a Golden Box in about two chests in five -, gold, a heavy pouch of extol, and dials (Axe, Flash, Breath or Eisen).
      • It is guarded: four divine soldiers and their commander. Two soldiers stand at the head of the stair, one keeps each wing, and the commander stands beside the throne. They attack on sight: whoever they see is their enemy, and a blow at one of them brings them all.
      • The divine soldiers are Enel's enforcers: bald, two small horns, long lobes hanging beside the face, a white robe with a red collar, dial skates under the feet - and they bleat. Their hands are empty: they fight with their dials.
        • Axe Dial: a blade of air thrown from the palm. You hardly see it, but it flies in a straight line, so step aside and it misses. At arm's length, the same palm cuts.
        • Flash Dial: held up, it glints for a moment, then bursts and blinds you for a few seconds - if you are looking at it. Look away while it glints.
        • Breath Dial: a gust that throws you a few blocks back, and does no harm.
        • And they glide to you on their skates.
      • They patrol the jungle too. Now and then - it is rare - two or three divine soldiers roam the Upper Yard, far from any sanctuary. They attack on sight there as well.
      • Their commander is a mountain of a man, twice their height, with ten Axe Dials on his sash. He announces each of his three attacks: he stands a moment, and cloud on the ground shows where it will fall.
        • Punch Mountain, at arm's length: straight before him, and it throws you a long way.
        • Stomach Mountain: he leaps and comes down belly first on a ring of cloud. Get out of the ring - right under him, the ten dials cut as well.
        • Drop Mountain, from afar: a flying kick along a thread of cloud. Step off the thread.
        • Make him miss and he stands open for a moment.
      • Three new blocks for builders, each with a recipe: the Shrine Plaque, the Thunder Drum and Cut Cloud Stairs.
    • Birka, Enel's homeland: a third kind of sky island. One Angel Island in five is now a Birkan island. Enel destroyed Birka: four of these islands in five are ravaged - and one in five was spared.
      • Scorched cloud under a veil of ash. A cloud island like an Angel Island, struck by lightning: plates of Ashen Cloud and Scorched Cloud, craters where the lightning fell, in clusters and of every size, and cracks running across the ground. The air is grey and ash drifts in it: you know the island from afar.
      • One great crater marks every ravaged island: fifty to ninety blocks across and about ten deep, its floor burnt black. You walk down into it and out again.
      • Nothing grows there, and nothing takes root in burnt cloud. Ashen Cloud and Scorched Cloud are dug with a shovel, like Island Cloud.
      • A town in ruins. Every ravaged island still carries what is left of its town: thirty to forty buildings in three rows round the great crater, in Birka's own style - cream plaster on a plinth of brick, friezes of indigo and baked clay, flat roofs, round windows. Low houses, stepped houses with a stair up to their terrace, tower houses three storeys high: every one broken towards the crater, each in its own way, with rooms you can still walk into. The first row stands on the crater's very rim, and the bowl cuts through it.
      • The temple stood on the rim too. The crater took a good part of it: what is left is cut open, one tower still up.
      • The vault. At the heart of the crater lies the temple's crypt, laid bare, and in it the treasure the town kept there: a rare dial every time (Flame, Impact or Reject), gold and gems, a heavy purse - and a fruit box one time in two.
      • Chests in the ruins. Nearly one building in two still holds a chest: what the fire left, a common dial now and then, and a rare dial about one time in nine. Until now only the dial shop of a village had Flame, Impact and Reject Dials.
      • The islands the lightning spared bear none of its marks: no crater, no burnt cloud, no ash. They have an Angel Island's land - white cloud, trees, a clear sky - and their town still stands.
      • A living Birkan town. The town as it was before the lightning: its temple on its square at the heart, lamp posts round it, and thirty to fifty houses in three rows - low houses, stepped houses with their terrace, tower houses three storeys high with a ladder from floor to floor. Every house is furnished and lived in, and has steps up to its door wherever the ground asks for them.
      • The Birkans. Townsfolk in pale robes with their town's frieze at the collar and the hem, and a Birkan's wings. They flee when struck.
      • The merchant, behind the counter of his shop before the temple's portal, sells Birka's masonry cheap and in number - plaster, brick, their slabs and stairs, the three friezes and the rose window, a few thousand extol a block - and two sorts of common dials. He also buys burnt cloud: hold Ashen or Scorched Cloud out to him and he pays 20,000 extol a block, 64 blocks at most from each merchant.
      • The craftsman of dials, in his workshop beside the shop, sells Flame Dials and Impact Dials and always has them: 1.5 to 2 million and 3 to 4 million extol.
      • The guards fight like the divine soldiers - empty hands, the Axe Dial in the palm, the Flash Dial, the Breath Dial, dial shoes - but they go for nobody on sight: you may walk the town freely. Strike a Birkan, open a chest of the town or break one of its walls, and every guard around turns on you - and the town remembers you, however far you go before coming back.
      • The crypt. Under the temple, down a stair that turns in its west tower, lies the town's vault, whole and guarded: the same treasure a ruined island's crater lays bare. Taking it is theft.
      • A modest chest in every house: provisions, a little gold, a common dial one time in ten. Never a rare dial.
      • Spawn eggs for the Birkan, the Birkan Guard, the Birkan Merchant and the Birkan Dial Craftsman are in the creative inventory.
      • Nine new blocks for builders: Birkan Plaster and its slab, Birkan Brick with its slab and stairs, the Chevron, Diamond and Cross Friezes, and the Birkan Rose Window. Plaster is burnt in a furnace from Ashen or Scorched Cloud - so it takes a Birkan island to build in its style - and the rest is crafted or cut from it. All are mined with a pickaxe.
      • In a world you already play, an Angel Island you have not visited yet may turn out to be Birkan, and one you have only partly explored may be Birkan in the parts you have not seen. A Birkan island you have already walked keeps what you saw of it: its town stands only in the parts you had not reached. No Upper Yard changes.
    • String Cloud, Swamp Cloud, Iron Cloud, the Iron Plate, the Heat Javelin and the Eisen Whip are in the creative inventory, for builders: none of them has a recipe.

    Changes

    • The ruins of Shandora hold far fewer Devil Fruit boxes: about one a city, where there were about four. The crypt gives one four times in ten per chest, a hall almost never, and a Golden Box turns up in about one city in six. The rest of the treasure - gold, gems, dials, extol - is unchanged.
    • No more bee nests in Skypiea. The trees of the Angel Islands no longer come with one, and neither does a sapling you grow there.

    Fixes

    • Sky Island no longer slows the server down. With the mod installed, every tick of the server lost about 30 milliseconds of the 50 it has, all the time and in every dimension, even if nobody had ever gone to Skypiea - enough for "Can't keep up!" to show as soon as anything else was going on. It was the search for boats fallen under the sea of clouds; it now costs nothing.
    • A Knock-Up Stream in eruption is seen from afar. The column of water now rises from the sea to the sky and shows as far as the land does - a geyser erupting is a landmark on the horizon. It used to be a few flakes, seen only from right beside it.
    • The warning shows too: during the half minute before an eruption, the sea heaves into a mound of foam over the geyser.
    • An eruption roars: a rush of air and water for as long as it lasts, heard about a hundred blocks away.

    0.3.0 (beta)¶

    Released 2026-10-02 · Download

    The biggest update so far. Weatheria, the weather scientists' floating island, with Haredas at home and weather for sale. Sky people of our own: Skypieans, White Berets and Shandias with their own faces and wings, and Shandias armed with bows, pistols, rifles and shields. The waver and the Milky Roads to travel between islands. A cloud sea you can see into, with fish in it. Three predators in the Upper Yards. Villages rebuilt, house by house. And Skypiea generates two to three times faster.

    Requires Minecraft 1.20.1, Forge 47+, Mine Mine no Mi 0.11.5 and AkumaLib 4.0.0 or newer (was 2.6.1). Client and server.

    ⚠️ Still a beta: worlds are generated from this version's rules. Chunks already explored keep what they have. Everything new that belongs to the land (Weatheria, the Milky Roads, the new houses, the solid island feet) appears only in places you have not been to yet.

    New: Weatheria

    • A floating island of four cloud tiers on a great glass bubble, green on top. Rare: about one for ten islands. It hangs over land as well as over the sea, and no geyser leads to it: fly there.
    • The scientists' town: Haredas's tower at the summit, pinwheel windmills, houses, the observatory's glass sphere, the Weather Ball garden and the balloon terminal, joined by white stairs with glass railings.
    • Paths and scenery: white promenades, lamp posts and benches facing the view, trimmed trees and cloud trees, flower beds, a snowy corner with its snowman, a sunny corner of sunflowers, a weather station.
    • Its people: old scientists in wizard robes and pointed hats, twelve looks. They keep to their homes and flee when struck.
    • Haredas, the master of the Art of Weather, as the anime shows him: blue robe, great white beard, floppy hat, small wings pointing down. He still teaches the Art of Weather, on Weatheria and on the climate terrace of the villages that have one (one village in three now).
    • Weather for sale: the Weather Vendor at the balloon terminal sells the weather of the Blue Sea below - clear sky (1,000,000 extol, 15 min), rain (1,500,000, 10 min) or a thunderstorm (3,000,000, 5 min). One sale a day at each booth.
    • Weather Cloud, a new block: it drizzles, snows or blows a breeze. Two cut clouds and a bucket of water make two; click one with a snowball, a feather or a bucket of water to change its weather.
    • Cloud foxes and sheep live on the island. Its chests hold a Devil Fruit box now and then.

    New: sky people of our own

    • Wings of our own: small round-feathered wings for the Skypieans, long swept-back ones for the Shandias. They fold at rest, open when walking and beat in the air. The Shandias no longer wear the Skypieans' antennae.
    • Skypiean villagers: eight people, men and women, young and old, with their hair grown into antennae. The dial merchant is one of them, behind his counter - and still sells the base mod's dials.
    • White Berets: eight faces under the same uniform, for guards and captains alike.

    New: armed Shandias

    • A warrior now carries one of six weapons: spear, bow, twin pistols, rifle, shield and spear, or bazooka. Each fights its own way: archers and gunmen keep their distance, the rifleman aims before he fires (you see his line of sight), the shield-bearer guards his front, walks ahead of the others and can send projectiles back.
    • Shandia Bow and Shandia Shield: two new items, usable by players.
    • The Shandia spear has a 3D model in hand, for players and warriors, and a redrawn icon.
    • Shandias never hit each other with their arrows and bullets.

    New: the waver and the Milky Roads

    • The waver, Skypiea's cloud-sea scooter: one rider, standing at the handlebars. Faster than a boat (about 13 blocks a second against 8), it glides on cloud as on the sea and climbs one-block steps.
      • Craft it with an iron ingot, planks and a Breath Dial, or buy it from the Waver Dealer at the seafront's dial workshop.
    • Milky Roads: every island is now linked to its nearest neighbour by a road of milky cloud, five blocks wide. It follows the sea where there is one and bridges the void where there is none, then climbs onto the island.

    New: life in the cloud sea

    • The cloud sea is translucent: you can see what swims in it.
    • Sky Fish in shoals, leaping out of the sea; the rare Balloon Sky Fish, floating with its back out; the Giant Sky Fish, whose fan cuts the surface; the Sky Lobster on the Angel Beaches. They drop fish.
    • A fishing rod works in the cloud sea: the bobber floats and fish bite.
    • Sea Cloud Bucket: an empty bucket takes a block of sea cloud, the full one lays it back, like powder snow. Sea cloud broken by hand no longer drops itself.
    • Sea cloud waters cloud farmland.

    New: the predators of the Upper Yards

    • The Cloud Wolf: a wolf on two legs, in packs of two or three. It hunts players on sight, and a wolf that is struck calls its pack. It can be tamed with cooked meat (one try in three): it then follows you, fights for you and sits when told. Three coats. Weaker than a Shandia, but faster.
    • The Sky Shark: a shark that swims the air, in twos and threes. It goes for blood: a wounded player draws it from far away, and it will even turn on a wounded shark.
    • The Sky Lamprey: a giant that waits sunk in the ground, with only its ring of teeth showing. Come too close and it rises, bites and holds you in its mouth for three seconds. Strike it hard - you or a friend - and it lets go. Rare, alone, and as tough as a Shandia chief.

    New: villages rebuilt

    • Five house shapes instead of three: the family house, a two-storey house with a balcony, the fisherman's house with its catch drying, a round house under a dome, a house with a gallery. Each draws its roof colour - turquoise, coral, amber or lilac - with shutters and a door painted to match, and its own walls.
    • Houses are furnished: table and chairs, a kitchen with a smoking chimney, beds, shelves, carpets, flowers at the windows.
    • The trades have their colours: an amber inn with a bar and rooms upstairs, a coral dial shop, a White Berets' post like a small white tower, a lilac dial workshop on the seafront.
    • Streets show: an oak border along the paving and lamp posts. Windows look like windows: cloud glass is redrawn with a wooden frame.
    • New blocks, all craftable: Coral, Amber and Lilac Shell Tiles and stairs; painted Shutters and Doors in four colours; Turquoise, Amber and Lilac Awning Cloth; Gilded Stone Bricks.
    • Seafront buildings now stand on their own stretch of beach instead of in the sea.

    For server builders

    • Knock-Up Stream block (creative only): placed anywhere, it erupts with the geysers and sends whoever stands on it to Skypiea, at the same place.
    • An empty Skypiea: empty_skypiea = true in inosky-server.toml gives a Skypiea with no sea, no island and no geyser, to build your own. Set it before the world is first opened.
    • Public biome tags for other mods and datapacks: inosky:is_skypiea, is_cloud_sea, is_angel_island, is_upper_yard.

    Changes

    • Skypiea generates two to three times faster. The land itself is unchanged.
    • Shandora gives about half the boxes it gave (4 a city instead of 8). The Golden Boxes are spared: more of the boxes you find are golden. This also reaches unopened chests in existing worlds.
    • Shandora's walls are inlaid with gilded stone bricks instead of blocks of gold. The crypt keeps its gold.
    • More lookouts and camps: an island gets a White Beret lookout wherever its shore has room, never beside another; an Upper Yard gets several Shandia camps wherever it has the clearings.
    • Compatibility checked: Terralith, and TerraBlender with Biomes O' Plenty - geysers and islands appear as usual.

    Fixes

    • An island's foot is solid cloud: under the waterline an island was made of sea cloud, which you could sink into - and which looked like glass.
    • The Shandias' hidden village no longer sits partly on an island.
    • White Beret guards defend the villagers every time: a guard could ignore an attack that happened while he was stepping down a block.
    • Leaves no longer block the Giant Jack's ramp.
    • A piece of roof no longer floats in Shandora's great hall, and village counters and tables no longer hover over the floor.
    • Valkyrien Skies: a ship arriving by geyser is never set down across a Milky Road.

    0.1.0 (beta)¶

    Released 2026-09-24 · Download

    The first release. A Forge addon for Mine Mine no Mi that adds Skypiea as a dedicated dimension, reached by riding a Knock-Up Stream up from the floor of the deep oceans: the cloud sea, the Angel Islands and their Skypiean villages, the beach, Heaven's Gate, and the Upper Yards with the Giant Jack, the ruins of Shandora and the Shandias.

    Requires Minecraft 1.20.1, Forge 47+, Mine Mine no Mi 0.11.5 and AkumaLib 2.5.0 or newer. Client and server.

    ⚠️ The base mod's own sky islands no longer generate in the overworld: Skypiea replaces them. Structures already generated in an existing world stay where they are.

    ⚠️ A beta: worlds are generated from this version's rules. A later version may change where islands and structures stand in chunks not yet explored.

    The way up: the Knock-Up Stream

    • A geyser on the floor of the deep oceans, one for each island above. Every ten minutes it rumbles for thirty seconds, then erupts for ninety (all configurable).
    • The column throws whatever is caught in it into the sky - players, mobs, dropped items, boats with their riders - and sends it on to Skypiea. An eruption always lasts long enough to carry somebody from the sea floor to the top.
    • You arrive on the ground or on the cloud sea, never inside a block: in front of Heaven's Gate on an island with a village.
    • /locate structure inosky:knock_up_stream finds one. /geyser tells where the nearest one is and when it erupts; /geyser now makes it erupt at once (operators).

    The way down

    • Step off an island and sink through the cloud sea: below the bottom of Skypiea you come out high above the same place in the overworld, with slow falling. Boats come back too.

    Skypiea

    • Islands only above the overworld's deep oceans; open cloud sea everywhere else above deep water, nothing above land.
    • The cloud sea: a sea of cloud you swim in, with a swell, bluish and animated so it is never mistaken for island cloud. Boats float and sail on it.
    • Its own sky, and no lava sea under it.
    • Island cloud, sea cloud and the Knock-Up Core are the dimension's own blocks; the Upper Yards are made of the overworld's earth and stone - the Vearth of the Blue Sea.

    Angel Islands

    Two islands in three: low islands of island cloud from top to bottom, a few trees rooted in the cloud. * Skypiean villages: houses of cloud brick, cut cloud and shell tiles, streets and a square, Skypiean civilians and traders (the base mod's own). Larger villages on the larger islands, with an inn, a bandstand, kitchen gardens and a cloud fountain. * Cloud farmland: a hoe tills island cloud, and crops grow in it exactly as in farmland. * The White Berets: their post in every village (with a rare chest of seized goods), recruits and a captain policing the village - calibrated against the base mod's NPCs. * The village blocks can be crafted: cloud brick, cut cloud, cloud pillars, shell tiles, awning cloth, cloud glass, conch lanterns, Vearth urns. * Angel Beach: the flat shore at the foot of the cliffs. Dials wash up on it over time. A seafront once per village island - the dial workshop, a promenade, a pier, deck chairs, sometimes a cloud castle - and one to three White Beret lookouts along the shore. * Heaven's Gate: the way into Skypiea on every village island - the gate, its tunnel, and a causeway to the promenade.

    Upper Yards

    One island in three, rarer and far larger: jungle plains around y 100, and massifs rising to about 215. * The Giant Jack (one Upper Yard in three): a beanstalk climbable to the top, a ramp up its whole height, and the Golden Bell at the summit with its chest - one or two boxes, a Golden Box in about two chests in five. * Shandora, the City of Gold (a little over half of the Upper Yards, sometimes beside a Giant Jack): its square, avenues and halls, the stepped temple and its altar, and the crypt under the Poneglyph - the treasure, one or two boxes a chest. Halls, altar and crypt all hold boxes now and then. * Small ruins of Shandora: one to three per Upper Yard in the jungle - an altar, a colonnade, a fallen house, a sacred well. * The Shandias: warriors and a chief guarding their land. They attack only looters - open their chests and they come for you. Their own weapons, the Shandia spear and the burn bazooka, and skills from dials (burn bazooka, impact, breath dash, sky step, the chief's reject). * Shandia war camps: two to four per Upper Yard, in clearings of the plains - four tipis round a fire, a totem, a weapon rack, a lookout. A chest in every tipi. * The hidden village: the Shandias' village in a pocket of cloud on the sea at the island's foot, opening on the sea - six tipis round a square, the statue of Kalgara, totems along the path, the chief by the fire.

    Compatibility

    • Worldgen mods: Skypiea reads the overworld's own biomes to find the deep oceans (minecraft:is_deep_ocean) and changes nothing in the overworld beyond the geyser.
    • Valkyrien Skies 2 (optional, 2.4.11 or newer): a ship in an erupting Knock-Up Stream rises to Skypiea with its crew, lands on open cloud sea and floats on it; a ship that falls out of Skypiea goes back to the overworld with its crew. Sky Island never requires VS.

    Configuration

    <world>/serverconfig/inosky-server.toml: * geyser: cycle_ticks (12000), eruption_ticks (1800), charge_ticks (600); * return: return_y (-32), the height below which a body falls back to the overworld; * angel_beach: dial_chance (2000), dial_cap (2), how often dials wash up and how many a stretch of beach holds.

    Known issues

    • An amethyst geode generated in a neighbouring chunk can crush a geyser's vent (#42).
